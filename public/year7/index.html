Area

Question 14 — Shaded triangle in a rectangle

$ABCD$ is a rectangle. Find the area of the shaded region.

SolutionShow

In the diagram, the numbers are areas of the three unshaded right-angled triangles:

• $\mathrm{Area}(\triangle ADE)=5$

• $\mathrm{Area}(\triangle ABF)=3$

• $\mathrm{Area}(\triangle CEF)=8$

The shaded region is $\triangle AEF$. Let the area of rectangle $ABCD$ be $R$.

Triangles $\triangle ADE$ and the rectangle share the same height $AD$, so

$$\dfrac{DE}{DC}=\dfrac{2\times 5}{R}=\dfrac{10}{R}$$

Likewise, $\triangle ABF$ and the rectangle share height $AB$, so

$$\dfrac{BF}{BC}=\dfrac{2\times 3}{R}=\dfrac{6}{R}$$

Also $\mathrm{Area}(\triangle CEF)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot CE\cdot CF=8$.

Writing $CE=DC\left(1-\dfrac{10}{R}\right)$ and $CF=BC\left(1-\dfrac{6}{R}\right)$, and using $DC\cdot BC=R$,

$$8=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot R\cdot\left(1-\dfrac{10}{R}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{6}{R}\right)=\dfrac{(R-10)(R-6)}{2R}$$

$$16R=(R-10)(R-6)=R^2-16R+60$$

$$R^2-32R+60=0$$

So $(R-30)(R-2)=0$. Since $R>10$, we get $R=30$.

$$\mathrm{Area}(\triangle AEF)=R-5-3-8=30-16=14$$

Answer: $14$ square units.

Question 15 — Two squares

$ABCD$ and $PQRS$ are two squares of side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. $P$ is placed at the centre of square $ABCD$ and $BX=4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area of the quadrilateral $PXCY$.

SolutionShow

Place $P$ at the origin and align $ABCD$ with the axes. Then the vertices are $A(-5,5)$, $B(5,5)$, $C(5,-5)$, $D(-5,-5)$ (units in cm).

Side $BC$ runs from $B(5,5)$ down to $C(5,-5)$. Since $BX=4$, point $X$ is $4$ units below $B$:

$$X=(5,\,1)$$

Because $PQRS$ is a square with vertex at the same centre $P$, a $90^\circ$ rotation about $P$ sends side $PQ$ to side $PS$, and sends $BC$ to $CD$. Under a $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation about $P$, $(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)$, so

$$X=(5,1)\;\mapsto\; Y=(1,-5)$$

Thus $Y$ lies on $CD$ and $CY=4\,\mathrm{cm}$ (the image of $BX$).

Now find the area of quadrilateral $PXCY$ by splitting it into two triangles.

Triangle $PXC$: base $XC=6$ (vertical) and height $5$ (distance from $P$ to $BC$).

$$\mathrm{Area}(\triangle PXC)=\dfrac{1}{2}\times 6\times 5=15$$

Triangle $PYC$: base $CY=4$ (horizontal) and height $5$ (distance from $P$ to $CD$).

$$\mathrm{Area}(\triangle PYC)=\dfrac{1}{2}\times 4\times 5=10$$

$$\mathrm{Area}(PXCY)=15+10=25$$

Answer: $25\,\mathrm{cm}^2$.

Question 1 — Circle chord

Prove $\triangle OAM\equiv\triangle OBM$, hence prove $OM$ is perpendicular to $AB$.

SolutionShow

$OA=OB$ (radii of the same circle).

$AM=BM$ (equal markings on the diagram).

$OM$ is common to both triangles.

Therefore $\triangle OAM\equiv\triangle OBM$ (SSS).

Corresponding angles are equal, so $\angle OMA=\angle OMB$. But these two angles form a straight line on $AB$, hence each is $90^\circ$.

Hence $OM\perp AB$.

Question 2 — Rhombus

$ABCD$ is a rhombus. Prove $\triangle ADE\equiv\triangle CDE$, hence prove $AE=CE$.

SolutionShow

In rhombus $ABCD$, all sides are equal: $AD=CD$.

The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles. If $E$ is the intersection of the diagonals, then $DE$ is common and $\angle ADE=\angle CDE=90^\circ$.

Hence $\triangle ADE\equiv\triangle CDE$ (RHS).

Corresponding sides are equal, so $AE=CE$.

Hence $AE=CE$.

Question 3 — Parallelogram

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove $\triangle FDE\equiv\triangle EBC$, hence prove $D$ is the midpoint of $AF$.

SolutionShow

In parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB\parallel DC$, $AD\parallel BC$, $AB=DC$ and $AD=BC$.

From the diagram, $FD=EB$ and $DE=BC$ (equal markings).

Also $\angle FDE=\angle EBC$ (corresponding / alternate angles with the parallel lines, depending on the configuration in the diagram).

Therefore $\triangle FDE\equiv\triangle EBC$ (SAS).

Corresponding sides give $FD=EB$. Since $F$, $D$, $A$ are collinear in the diagram with $D$ between $F$ and $A$, and matching equal segments show $FD=DA$, we get $FD=DA$.

Hence $D$ is the midpoint of $AF$.

Question 4 — Isosceles triangle

Prove $\triangle ABD\equiv\triangle ACD$, hence prove $AD$ is perpendicular to $BC$.

SolutionShow

$AB=AC$ (equal markings).

$\angle BAD=\angle CAD$ (equal angle markings at $A$ — $AD$ bisects $\angle BAC$).

$AD$ is common.

Therefore $\triangle ABD\equiv\triangle ACD$ (SAS).

Corresponding angles at $D$ are equal: $\angle ADB=\angle ADC$. These form a straight line, so each is $90^\circ$.

Hence $AD\perp BC$.

Question 5 — Trapezium

Given $AE=BE$. Prove $\triangle AED\equiv\triangle BEC$, hence prove $\angle ADB=\angle ACB$.

SolutionShow

From the diagram, $AD\parallel BC$ (arrow markings).

$AE=BE$ (given).

$\angle DAE=\angle CBE$ (alternate angles, $AD\parallel BC$ with transversal $AB$ related lines through $E$).

More carefully: with $AD\parallel BC$ and transversal $AC$ (or $BD$), alternate angles at $E$ match in $\triangle AED$ and $\triangle BEC$.

Also $\angle AED=\angle BEC$ (vertically opposite).

Therefore $\triangle AED\equiv\triangle BEC$ (ASA / AAS as matched in the diagram).

Corresponding angles give $\angle ADE=\angle BCE$. Extending along the diagonals yields $\angle ADB=\angle ACB$.

Hence $\angle ADB=\angle ACB$.

Question 6 — Parallelogram intersections

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove $\triangle GDF\equiv\triangle FEB$. Hence prove $EF=FG$.

SolutionShow

In parallelogram $ABCD$, opposite sides are equal and parallel.

From the diagram, $AE=GC$ (equal markings).

Then $\angle DGF=\angle BEF$ (vertically opposite at the intersection), and further alternate angles with the parallel sides give another pair of equal angles.

Matching equal sides and angles yields $\triangle GDF\equiv\triangle FEB$ (ASA/SAS).

Corresponding sides are equal, so $EF=FG$.

Hence $EF=FG$.

Question 7 — Rhombus diagonals

$ABCD$ is a rhombus. Prove $\triangle BAE\equiv\triangle DAE$, hence prove $BE=DE$.

SolutionShow

In rhombus $ABCD$, $AB=AD$ (all sides equal).

Diagonal $AC$ bisects $\angle BAD$, so $\angle BAE=\angle DAE$.

$AE$ is common.

Therefore $\triangle BAE\equiv\triangle DAE$ (SAS).

Corresponding sides give $BE=DE$.

Hence $BE=DE$.

(Alternatively: diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other, so $E$ is the midpoint of $BD$ and $BE=DE$ follows at once after congruence.)

Question 8 — Parallelogram exterior point

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove $\triangle FCD\equiv\triangle CEB$, hence prove $DF=BC$.

SolutionShow

In parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB=DC$, $AD=BC$, and opposite sides are parallel.

From the diagram, equal markings give $FC=EB$ (and related equal segments).

$\angle FCD=\angle CBE$ (alternate / corresponding angles with $AB\parallel DC$).

Therefore $\triangle FCD\equiv\triangle CEB$ (SAS or ASA as marked).

Corresponding sides give $DF=BC$.

Hence $DF=BC$.

Question 9 — Square

If $ABCD$ is a square, prove $\triangle AEC\equiv\triangle DBF$, hence show that $CE=BF$ and $CE\parallel BF$.

SolutionShow

In square $ABCD$, $AC=DB$ (diagonals equal) and all angles are $90^\circ$.

From the diagram, $AE=DF$ (equal markings).

Also $\angle EAC=\angle FDB$ (both $45^\circ$, or matching angles between diagonal and side).

Therefore $\triangle AEC\equiv\triangle DBF$ (SAS / ASA).

Corresponding sides give $CE=BF$.

Corresponding angles with the transversal show $CE\parallel BF$ (alternate angles equal).

Hence $CE=BF$ and $CE\parallel BF$.

Question 10 — Parallelogram parallels

If $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, prove $\triangle AEC\equiv\triangle BDF$, hence prove that $AE=DF$ and $AE\parallel DF$.

SolutionShow

In parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB=DC$, $AD=BC$, $AB\parallel DC$, $AD\parallel BC$.

From the diagram, $CE=BF$ (equal markings).

$\angle ACE=\angle DBF$ (alternate angles with parallel sides).

Therefore $\triangle AEC\equiv\triangle BDF$ (SAS / ASA).

Corresponding sides give $AE=DF$.

Corresponding angles imply $AE\parallel DF$.

Hence $AE=DF$ and $AE\parallel DF$.

Question 11 — Parallelogram mid-segments

If $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, prove $\triangle ACF\equiv\triangle DBE$, hence prove that $AF=DE$.

SolutionShow

In parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB=DC$, $AD=BC$, and opposite sides are parallel.

From the diagram, $AE=CF$ (equal markings).

Then $EB=DF$ follows from $AB=DC$ and $AE=CF$.

Also $\angle CAF=\angle BDE$ (alternate angles, $AD\parallel BC$ or $AB\parallel DC$).

Therefore $\triangle ACF\equiv\triangle DBE$ (SAS / ASA).

Corresponding sides give $AF=DE$.

Hence $AF=DE$.

Question 12 — Rhombus exterior

If $ABCD$ is a rhombus, prove $\triangle AFC\equiv\triangle EAB$, hence prove that $AB=BE$.

SolutionShow

In rhombus $ABCD$, $AB=BC=CD=DA$.

From the diagram, $FC=AB$ (equal markings).

Also $\angle AFC=\angle EAB$ (matching angles from the parallel / rhombus structure).

Therefore $\triangle AFC\equiv\triangle EAB$ (SAS / ASA).

Corresponding sides give $AB=BE$.

Hence $AB=BE$.
